# Data sources and licences

Orya Halo is non-commercial and display-only. Every source below was checked on
**2026-11-14** against the provider's own documentation and terms (the traffic cameras added
after Digitraffic and Hong Kong on **2026-11-15**). The same facts live in code:
each plugin in `packages/sources` carries a `licence` and `attribution`, and the gateway
serves its upstreams' terms at `/v1/attribution`. If this page and the code disagree, fix both.

The rule: if a source forbids public display, we do not use it. If it forbids redistribution,
its data never passes through the gateway; the browser fetches it directly.

## Aircraft models

The 3D aircraft are generated in code from published dimensions (length, wingspan, fuselage
width, wing sweep, engine layout), which are facts rather than anyone's design. No third-party
3D model, livery artwork or logo is used, so there is nothing to license or credit.

They are painted in simplified airline colour schemes (`packages/schema/src/aircraft-liveries.ts`):
a few flat colours per airline (fuselage top and belly, fin, engines, and for some the nose or
the fin's colour carried onto the tail cone), chosen by eye as an impression of how its
aircraft look from a distance. Small on screen, the fin is drawn thicker and dark colours
brighter so the colour can be seen at all, and the flat aircraft symbols further out carry one
of these colours on their tail. No logo, name, emblem, typeface or artwork
is drawn, the schemes are not copies of any livery, and no airline made or endorsed them. The
airline is read from the flight's callsign; nothing else about the airline is shown.

## Vessel models

The 3D ships are generic hull forms generated in code (`packages/layers/src/vessels/vessel-mesh.ts`),
one per kind of vessel (container ship, tanker, bulk carrier, car carrier, cruise ship, ferry,
general cargo ship, fishing vessel, tug, pilot boat, patrol vessel, sailing and motor yachts,
and a generic workboat), stretched to each ship's length and beam as its AIS static data reports
them. They are no particular ship or design, and no third-party 3D model is used.

They are painted in simplified colour schemes (`packages/schema/src/vessel-schemes.ts`): colours
by type of vessel, a few for roles (coast guard, search and rescue, naval grey, research), and
for some container lines, car carriers, cruise lines and ferry operators a few flat colours
(hull, superstructure, funnel, the share of containers in the line's colour), chosen by eye as
an impression of how their ships look from a distance. No logo, name, emblem, typeface, hull
lettering or artwork is drawn, the schemes are not copies of any livery, and no operator made or
endorsed them. The operator is read from the ship's broadcast name, and only where a fleet's
naming identifies it beyond doubt ("MAERSK …", "MSC …", "EVER …", "… OF THE SEAS"), and only for
the kind of ship that fleet sails (an "MSC …" tanker gets a tanker's colours). Lines whose ship
names do not say who operates them, such as Hapag-Lloyd, are left in the colours of the type.

## Share-alike

`apps/web/public/data/cables.bin` and the base map extract are databases derived from
OpenStreetMap, and aircraft positions relayed by the gateway derive from adsb.lol. All three
are offered under the [ODbL 1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/), not under
this repository's MIT licence, which covers the code only.

## Cameras

Only cameras that a public authority publishes as open data are included. Orya Halo never
indexes unsecured or private camera feeds.
