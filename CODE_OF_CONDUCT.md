# Code of conduct

Orya Halo is built by people who want to see the planet's traffic, weather and shakes as they
happen. We want everyone who takes part, in issues, pull requests, discussions or anywhere else
the project lives, to be able to do that without being made to feel small.

## What we expect

- Be kind and assume good intent. Most mistakes are honest ones.
- Criticise work, not people. Say what is wrong and, where you can, what would be better.
- Welcome newcomers. Explain terms, link to the docs, and remember you were new once.
- Accept that a maintainer may say no to a change, and say why when you are the maintainer.
- Respect people's time and privacy. Do not share someone's personal details without their
  consent.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, threats or slurs, in public or in private messages.
- Unwelcome sexual attention or imagery.
- Attacks on someone's identity, background or ability.
- Repeatedly pushing a point after you have been asked to stop.
- Using the project to track or expose a particular person, for example by following a private
  aircraft or vessel to harm or harass the people on board.

## Reporting a problem

Contact the maintainer privately, using the contact details on the
[maintainer's GitHub profile](https://github.com/dkampouridis), with what happened, where and when, and links or screenshots if
you have them. Reports are read by the maintainers only and kept confidential. You will get
an answer, normally within a week. If a maintainer is involved, say so, and they will not
take part in handling the report.

## What happens next

The maintainers decide what to do based on what happened. That can be a private word, a
public note on the thread, removal of comments, or a temporary or permanent ban from the
project's spaces. We will tell the person who reported it what we did, as far as we can
without breaking someone else's privacy.

This applies to every space the project runs, and to people representing the project
elsewhere.
